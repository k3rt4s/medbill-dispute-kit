# New Hampshire state pack

New Hampshire legal sources and verification gaps for medical-billing disputes.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify before use, including later session laws and applicability to the service date. Unresolved topics remain explicit gaps.

## Hospital itemization right

[RSA 151:12-a](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XI/151/151-12-a.htm), verified 2026-09-07, requires a medical provider receiving third-party payment to supply the patient and third party an itemized statement within 30 days of service. It permits a nonitemized bill if prominently offering free itemization on request. Do not convert this into a universal 30-day deadline after a request or omit the third-party-payment condition.

## Unfair claims settlement practices

[RSA 417:4, XV](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/xxxvii/417/417-4.htm), verified 2026-09-07, identifies practices such as misrepresentation and failing to act promptly on claims communications, subject to its without-just-cause and nonaccidental conditions. The private remedy below has an administrative prerequisite; this is not an unrestricted right to sue over every alleged claims violation.

## Bad-faith remedy

[RSA 417:19](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXVII/417/417-19.htm), verified 2026-09-07, permits an adversely affected consumer's damages action following a final violation finding or cease-and-desist order in a proceeding brought by the insurance commissioner. Its separate inaction/appeal procedure must not be mistaken for an automatic merits finding against an insurer. A broader common-law bad-faith theory, damages and demand requirements remain unverified. Application to employer plans requires legal review under [29 U.S.C. § 1144](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1144), verified 2026-09-07.

## State surprise-billing statute

[RSA 420-J:8-e](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXVII/420-J/420-J-8-e.htm), verified 2026-09-07, establishes a state provider/carrier fair-value dispute process tied to Chapter 358-T balance-billing prohibitions. Do not instruct a patient to initiate provider/carrier arbitration. This pack verifies the ground-ambulance prohibition below; the precise emergency/nonemergency scope of the other Chapter 358-T sections remains a gap. Federal protections are explained in the [CMS insured-patient guide](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights/know-your-rights-insurance), verified 2026-09-07.

## Ground-ambulance protection

[RSA 358-T:5](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXI/358-T/358-T-5.htm), effective January 1, 2026 and verified 2026-09-07, bars charging a covered person with covered ground-ambulance benefits beyond plan cost-sharing, whether the provider participates or not. It expressly excepts scheduled inter-facility transfers by a nonparticipating provider satisfying the referenced federal notice-and-consent criteria. Read the [definitions in RSA 358-T:1](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XXXI/358-T/358-T-1.htm), verified 2026-09-07, and confirm the plan, service date and exception before using this protection. Federal NSA protection generally excludes ground ambulance, as the CMS source above explains.

## Small claims court

[RSA 503:1](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LI/503/503-1.htm), verified 2026-09-07, defines small claims up to $10,000 excluding interest and costs, not involving real-estate title. It includes a jury-demand/transfer mechanism for amounts exceeding $1,500; small claims cannot be promised to remain informal. ## Statute of limitations

[RSA 508:4](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/LII/508/508-4.htm), verified 2026-09-07, generally gives three years for personal actions, subject to exceptions and its discovery provision. This is consistent with the kit's NH contract 3/3 row, not a case-specific accrual determination. Have any exception, acknowledgment or payment effect reviewed before calculating a deadline.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

A New Hampshire-specific medical-debt reporting prohibition remains unverified. Federal [15 U.S.C. § 1681i](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i), verified 2026-09-07, provides a mechanism for disputing report accuracy; it does not establish a blanket medical-debt reporting ban.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

[RSA 151:12-b](https://gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/XI/151/151-12-b.htm), verified 2026-09-07, limits hospital self-pay charges to the amount generally billed and received for insured patients' corresponding service. This is a pricing rule, not automatic free care or a specified FAP income threshold. Additional state FAP requirements remain unverified. Covered charitable hospitals also have federal policy, charge and collection requirements under [26 U.S.C. § 501(r)(4)-(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/501), verified 2026-09-07.

## Hospital lien statute

[RSA 448-A:1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-hampshire/title-xli/chapter-448-a/section-448-a-1/), verified from the 2025 statutory mirror on 2026-09-07, gives licensed hospitals and specified home-health providers a lien for reasonable and necessary charges on the patient's recovery from another for accidental injuries outside workers' compensation. Notice, perfection, priority and any later amendments require review before challenging a particular lien.

## Key New Hampshire-specific advantages

1. The statutory itemization provision supplies a concrete request basis within its third-party-payment scope.
2. The hospital self-pay pricing limit supplies a comparison basis distinct from financial-assistance eligibility.
3. The 2026 ground-ambulance rule addresses a federal NSA gap, subject to its coverage and transfer conditions.
4. A final insurance administrative finding can support the conditional consumer remedy under RSA 417:19.

These are practical uses of the dated sources above, not guarantees of recovery.
