# Montana state pack

Montana legal sources and verification gaps for medical-billing disputes.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify before using a citation in a letter or filing. Unresolved sections below require local verification; they do not establish that Montana has no applicable law.

## Hospital itemization right

A current statewide itemized-bill duty and response deadline could not be verified. Request itemization, but leave the statutory deadline as [VERIFIED ITEMIZATION DEADLINE]. A related, distinct right is an estimate on request for recommended or current services exceeding $500, provided when scheduled or within 10 business days of request. The estimate is not binding and may be requested in writing or electronically. This does not establish a post-service itemization deadline. Source: [MCA § 50-4-512](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0500/chapter_0040/part_0050/section_0120/0500-0040-0050-0120.html), verified 2026-09-07.

## Unfair claims settlement practices

[MCA § 33-18-201](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0330/chapter_0180/part_0020/section_0010/0330-0180-0020-0010.html) lists prohibited claims practices. A civil remedy exists for only the specified subsections identified in § 33-18-242 below; do not treat every listed practice as a separate private cause of action. Sources verified 2026-09-07.

## Bad-faith remedy

[MCA § 33-18-242](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0330/chapter_0180/part_0020/section_0420/0330-0180-0020-0420.html), verified 2026-09-07, permits an independent claim for actual damages from violations of § 33-18-201(1), (4), (5), (6), (9), or (13), without proving a general business practice. It preserves specified contract/fraud remedies but bars a separate bad-faith claim for claim handling. A reasonable legal or factual basis for contesting the claim is a defense. The insured's statutory deadline is two years from the violation; a third-party claimant must first obtain settlement or judgment and has one year from that event. Do not substitute the general contract period for these deadlines.

Plan type matters: [29 U.S.C. § 1144](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/1144) contains ERISA preemption, insurance-saving and deemer provisions (verified 2026-09-07). Application to an individual claim requires legal review; this pack does not promise a state remedy for an employer plan.

## State surprise-billing statute

Whether Montana adds broader protection remains a verification gap. The federal No Surprises Act protects covered emergency services and certain out-of-network services at in-network facilities; scope and exceptions matter. Use the [CMS insured-patient explanation](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights/know-your-rights-insurance), verified 2026-09-07, and verify the service and plan before asserting protection.

## Ground-ambulance protection

Montana-specific protection was not resolved. CMS states ground ambulance generally falls outside federal NSA billing protection, subject to state law. Do not infer that Montana has none. Source: [CMS ground-ambulance discussion](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights/know-your-rights-insurance), verified 2026-09-07.

## Small claims court

The [Justice Court of Madison County's small-claims page](https://madisoncountymt.gov/286/Small-Claims), as published on 2026-09-07, gives a $7,000 maximum recovery and a $30 filing fee. Those are the local court's published figures, not a promise about additional service charges or another county's current fee. The page says parties may not have an attorney unless both are represented. It also specifies local demand and venue requirements. Verify the filing county's instructions before filing. Montana DOJ's [guide](https://www.dojmt.gov/office-of-consumer-protection/small-claims-court/) identifies the justice-court small-claims process under Title 25, Chapter 35 (verified 2026-09-07).

## Statute of limitations

[MCA § 27-2-202](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0270/chapter_0020/part_0020/section_0020/0270-0020-0020-0020.html), verified 2026-09-07, states six years for an obligation founded on a written instrument and five for a contract/account/promise not founded on one. It separately gives three years for other nonwritten obligations. The kit's existing SOL index says eight years for written contracts; that conflict is recorded for integration. The current code includes a 2025 amendment. Applicability to older accrued claims and transitional rules remain unverified, so do not calculate a patient's deadline from this summary alone.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

A Montana-specific ban was not verified. Do not assert either its existence or absence. Federal reinvestigation rights appear in [15 U.S.C. § 1681i](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i), verified 2026-09-07. That dispute mechanism is not a blanket prohibition on reporting medical debt.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

[MCA § 50-5-121](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0500/chapter_0050/part_0010/section_0210/0500-0050-0010-0210.html), verified 2026-09-07, requires written financial-assistance and community-benefit policies for hospitals operating as nonprofit facilities, and adherence/public availability as specified in subsection (3). The source displays both the version effective through December 31, 2026 and a January 1, 2027 version; use the one applicable to the event. No statewide income threshold is supplied here. Federal [26 U.S.C. § 501(r)(4)-(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/501) also addresses assistance policies, charges and collection requirements for covered charitable hospitals (verified 2026-09-07).

## Hospital lien statute

[MCA § 71-3-1114](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0140/0710-0030-0110-0140.html), verified 2026-09-07, provides liens following required notice on specified third-party injury claims, judgments and settlements, and separately on specified insurance proceeds/payments. Do not describe this as limited solely to a tort settlement. Notice, exclusions, priorities and enforceability in a particular case require review of the rest of Part 11; they are not resolved here.

## Key Montana-specific advantages

1. Request a written service estimate under § 50-4-512, using its stated scope rather than claiming an itemization deadline.
2. Consider the specified private insurer remedy in § 33-18-242, which does not require proof of a general business practice.
3. Request the nonprofit hospital's publicly available assistance policy under § 50-5-121 and compare its actions to that policy.
4. Use the justice-court small-claims process where the local eligibility and procedural rules fit the claim.

These are practical uses of the dated sources above, not guarantees of recovery.
