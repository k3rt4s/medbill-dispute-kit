# South Dakota state pack, research draft

The state-law layer for South Dakota patients. This concise draft states only items verified from sources listed below as of 2026-09-07. A gap is not an assertion that South Dakota lacks a protection.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07.

## Hospital itemization right

South Dakota requires a health-care provider or facility to disclose **all fees and charges for health-care procedures upon a patient's request**. SDCL § 34-12E-8. This source does not supply a verified post-service itemized-statement format or response deadline. Request the itemized bill and this disclosure in writing. [SDCL § 34-12E-8](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/34-12E-8).

## Unfair claims settlement practices

For insurers, SDCL § 58-12-34 identifies claim-handling conduct including policy/fact misrepresentation, failure to acknowledge communications promptly, failure to adopt reasonable investigation standards, unreasonable denial, and failure to seek prompt, fair, equitable settlement where liability and causation are reasonably clear. The administrative unfair-claims standard applies when conduct is flagrant/conscious or frequent enough to show a general business practice. SDCL § 58-12-33. The Director issues the stated cease-and-desist order; the Secretary of Labor and Regulation may impose the specified penalties under § 58-12-36. [SDCL § 58-12-33](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/58-12-33); [§ 58-12-34](https://sdlegislature.gov/Statutes/58-12-34); [§ 58-12-36](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/58-12-36.html).

## Bad-faith remedy, statute or common law

South Dakota recognizes first-party insurance bad faith as an intentional tort in the insured-insurer relationship. The Supreme Court describes the core proof as no reasonable basis for denying benefits and the insurer's knowledge of, or reckless disregard for, that lack of a basis; an insurer may challenge a fairly debatable claim. *Harvieux v. Progressive Northern Insurance Co.*, 2018 S.D. 52, ¶¶ 13, 21. This is an insurer-side theory and is not a claim against the provider merely for issuing a bill. [South Dakota Supreme Court opinion](https://ujs.sd.gov/media/4nrj3tsq/28159xs9ktjf.pdf).

## State surprise-billing statute, if broader than the federal NSA

The South Dakota Division of Insurance's published consumer material describes the federal No Surprises Act. This research did not verify an enacted South Dakota patient-side statute broader than that federal protection. Use the federal analysis for the federal categories and confirm plan type and current state law before making a state-specific balance-billing claim. [Division of Insurance newsletter, pp. 1-3](https://dlr.sd.gov/insurance/publications/newsletters/newsletter_summer22.pdf).

## Ground-ambulance protection

The state rule directly verified in this research, ARSD 67:16:25:08, prescribes certain ground-ambulance billing requirements in the Medicaid-rule context; it does not state a general patient balance-billing ban. The Legislature's 2026 index records that SB 211, which proposed an emergency out-of-network ground-ambulance balance-billing restriction, failed on a Senate floor vote on February 24, 2026. A failed bill is not operative law. The broader South Dakota patient protection remains a verification gap. [ARSD 67:16:25:08](https://sdlegislature.gov/Rules/Administrative/67%3A16%3A25%3A08); [2026 Legislative Index, SB 211](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/LegislativeIndex/286219.pdf?Year=2026).

**South Dakota Attorney General, Division of Consumer Protection.** It investigates deceptive or misleading business and trade practices and may pursue enforcement for the State; it does not serve as a consumer's private attorney. Contact: 1302 E. Highway 14, Suite 3, Pierre, SD 57501; 605-773-4400; 1-800-300-1986 (South Dakota only); `consumerhelp@state.sd.us`. ## Small claims court

**Court and limit:** South Dakota small claims court handles claims up to **$12,000** under SDCL § 16-12C-13 and is designed as an informal procedure. **Fees:** the judiciary's schedule effective July 12, 2026 lists total filing/court/postage charges of $24.84 for $0.01-$100, $30.84 for the published $100-$1,000 band (confirm the overlapping $100 boundary with the clerk), $40.84 for $1,000.01-$3,999.99, and $42.84 for $4,000-$12,000; confirm additional-defendant charges with the clerk. **Attorney rule:** either side may use an attorney, though the judiciary says one is not necessary. [UJS small-claims information](https://ujs.sd.gov/media/0n5pu4xg/small-claims-court.pdf); [UJS fee schedule](https://ujs.sd.gov/media/zqnnr2vz/small-claims-filing-fee-07_12_2026.pdf).

## Statute of limitations

SDCL § 15-2-13 supplies a **six-year** period after accrual for an action on an express or implied contract, subject to the statute's stated exceptions. That matches the kit's South Dakota row: six years for written contracts and open accounts. The same row marks partial payment and written acknowledgment as re-aging events; this research did not independently verify those re-aging conclusions, so they are not adopted here. [SDCL § 15-2-13](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/15-2-13.html).

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

This research did not directly verify a South Dakota-specific hospital financial-assistance-policy mandate. Federal IRC § 501(r)(4) remains relevant to tax-exempt hospital organizations, which must establish a written financial-assistance policy; it does not automatically cover every provider. Request the particular hospital's policy and application. [IRS financial-assistance-policy guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policies-faps).

## Hospital lien statute

For care connected to an injury where the patient asserts or maintains a damages claim against another, SDCL § 44-12-1 gives a South Dakota licensed hospital a lien on the patient's recovery, judgment, compromise, or settlement for reasonable and necessary charges. Before filing/enforcing, § 44-12-3.1 requires submission to available third-party health benefits in the circumstances stated there. The chapter specifies filing and notice mechanics at §§ 44-12-4 through 44-12-8. This is an injury-recovery lien, not a finding about ordinary collection against other assets. [SDCL ch. 44-12](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/44-12.html); [2019 Session Law ch. 195, § 1](https://mylrc.sdlegislature.gov/api/Documents/54642.pdf?Year=2019).

## Key South Dakota-specific advantages

1. **Requested fee disclosure.** SDCL § 34-12E-8 supports a written request for all procedure fees and charges.
2. **Concrete insurer-complaint standards.** SDCL §§ 58-12-33-.36 specify claim practices and give the insurance regulator an enforcement route.
3. **Insured bad-faith claim.** The Supreme Court recognizes an intentional-tort claim when an insurer denies benefits without a reasonable basis and with the required knowledge or recklessness.
4. **Accessible small claims process.** The court system publishes a $12,000 limit, simple procedure, attorney rule, and current fee bands.
5. **Injury-settlement lien check.** A hospital lien is tied to an injury recovery and has third-party-payor and filing/notice conditions worth checking against the particular lien demand.

## Verification gaps

- **Post-service itemization.** The verified statute requires fee/charge disclosure upon request, but did not establish a general itemized-bill format, delivery deadline, or private remedy.
- **Surprise billing and ground ambulance.** The state source reviewed describes the federal NSA. The verified state ambulance rule does not state a broad patient balance-billing protection; proposed SB 211 failed. No broader enacted protection is asserted or negated.
- **Medical-debt credit reporting.** The state index search returned no South Dakota row, and HB 1210 was only a bill source. Current enacted law was not verified.
- **State FAP mandate.** Federal § 501(r) is verified for tax-exempt hospitals. A separate South Dakota mandate was not directly verified.
- **SOL re-aging.** The six-year limitations period matches the kit row; the row's payment/written-acknowledgment re-aging flags were not independently verified.
