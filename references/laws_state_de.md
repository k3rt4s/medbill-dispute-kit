# Delaware state pack

The state-law layer for Delaware patients. All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify before relying on a citation in a letter or court filing.

## Hospital itemization right

- **General hospital rule:** No general Delaware hospital itemization-on-demand statute was verified in the sources reviewed. Do not represent that Delaware gives every hospital patient a statutory itemized-bill deadline.
- **Narrow facility rule:** **16 Del. C. § 2220(3)** requires a treatment facility to send an itemized statement, in plain language, at least every 3 calendar months after admission. This section is within Delaware's involuntary-treatment chapter and should not be cited as a general-hospital billing rule. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 16 PDF, § 2220(3)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title16/title16.pdf).
- **Narrow workers' compensation rule:** **19 Del. C. § 2322B(i)(5)(b)** requires a hospital's workers' compensation bill to use a nationally recognized uniform billing-code format with enough detail to document the services or items. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 19, § 2322B](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title19/c023/sc02/index.html).
- **Practical use:** Request an itemized bill in writing, but use the provider's billing policy and the actual account documents rather than asserting an unverified statewide deadline.

## Unfair claims settlement practices

- **Statute:** **18 Del. C. § 2304(16)**, within Delaware's Unfair Trade Practices Act.
- **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 18, Chapter 23, § 2304](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title18/c023/index.html).
- **Substance:** The statute treats as unfair claim-settlement practices, when committed frequently enough to show a general business practice, conduct including misrepresenting policy provisions, failing to acknowledge communications promptly, failing to investigate reasonably, refusing payment without reasonable investigation, failing to affirm or deny coverage in a reasonable time, and failing to attempt a prompt, fair, and equitable settlement when liability is reasonably clear.
- **Use:** Use this citation in a complaint to the Insurance Commissioner. The chapter gives the Commissioner investigative, hearing, cease-and-desist, and penalty authority under **18 Del. C. §§ 2306-2308**. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 18, Chapter 23, §§ 2306-2308](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title18/c023/index.html).

## Bad-faith remedy, statute or common law

- **Common-law rule:** Delaware recognizes a first-party insured's claim for bad-faith delay or nonpayment as a breach of contractual obligations. The insurer's lack of reasonable justification is the relevant standard.
- **Authority:** *Tackett v. State Farm Fire & Casualty Insurance Co.*, **653 A.2d 254, 262-64 (Del. 1995)**. Delaware's Civil Pattern Jury Instructions identify *Tackett* as authority for the first-party duty and the no-reasonable-justification standard. **Source:** [Delaware Civil Pattern Jury Instructions, §§ 17.6 and 17.10](https://courts.delaware.gov/forms/download.aspx?id=85928).
- **Limits stated by the decision:** *Tackett* held that emotional-distress damages were unavailable there without accompanying physical injury; it also explained that punitive damages may be available for an intentional or malicious insurance-contract breach. Do not treat that case as a fixed statutory penalty or a substitute for advice on an ERISA-covered plan.

## State surprise-billing statute, if broader than the federal NSA

- **Emergency care:** **18 Del. C. § 3565(b)** requires the described Delaware policies to cover emergency care from non-network providers at a negotiated or agreed rate and bars balance billing when the provider-insurer rate dispute goes to the Commissioner's arbitration process. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 18, § 3565](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title18/c035/sc03/).
- **Nonemergency network disclosure:** **18 Del. C. § 3571S(d)** requires specified disclosures and consent for nonemergency out-of-network services. If the provider fails to give the required disclosure or obtain the timely signed consent, the provider may not balance bill the covered person for services not covered by the policy; an estimate requested from the provider is due in writing within 3 business days. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 18, § 3571S](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title18/c035/sc03/).
- **Scope caution:** These state provisions apply to the policy types described in their text. The federal No Surprises Act remains the federal protection for plans and services within its scope; do not assume Delaware's provisions reach a self-funded employer plan without checking the plan and current law.

## Ground-ambulance protection

- **No statewide ground-ambulance balance-billing protection was verified for this pack.** Delaware's own **18 Del. C. § 3571H(c)** says the balance-billing limitations in § 3348 do not apply to emergency medical services in that section when furnished by volunteer fire companies or certified emergency-medical-services provider agencies. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 18, § 3571H](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title18/c035/sc03/).
- **Federal context:** CMS states that ground ambulance services generally are not covered by the No Surprises Act's billing protections unless state law supplies a different rule. **Source:** [CMS, Know your medical bill rights](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights).
- **Practical use:** For a ground-ambulance bill, preserve the bill and insurance explanation of benefits, then verify the provider type and any local rule before alleging a balance-billing violation.

## Small claims court

- **Court name:** Justice of the Peace Court, civil jurisdiction.
- **Jurisdictional limit:** **$25,000** for the covered contract and property-related actions under **10 Del. C. § 9301**. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 10, § 9301](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title10/c093/index.html).
- **Debt-claim filing fee, as published on 2026-09-07:** $35 below $1,000, $40 from $1,000 through $5,000, and $45 above $5,000. **Source:** [Delaware Justice of the Peace Court civil fees](https://courts.delaware.gov/help/fees/jpfees.aspx).
- **Attorney rule:** An individual may appear without an attorney; the Court says a person who is unfamiliar with the legal issues or procedures may wish to consult one. **Source:** [Delaware Justice of the Peace Court civil proceedings](https://courts.delaware.gov/jpcourt/jpcivil.aspx).

## Statute of limitations

- **Written and oral / non-sealed contract claims:** The kit's existing Delaware row states **3 years** for both written and oral contracts. That is consistent with **10 Del. C. § 8106(a)**, which imposes a 3-year period for, among other things, a debt not evidenced by a record or instrument under seal and an action based on a promise. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 10, § 8106](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title10/c081/).
- **Important qualification:** Section 8106(c) separately allows a written contract involving at least $100,000 to specify a period of up to 20 years. Do not apply the three-year entry mechanically where that statutory exception, a seal, tolling, or another claim type may matter.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

- **Statute:** Delaware Medical Debt Protection Act, **6 Del. C. § 2507J**.
- **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 6, Chapter 25J, § 2507J](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c025j/index.html).
- **Substance:** No person may report medical debt to a consumer reporting agency, and a consumer reporting agency may not make a consumer report it knows or should know contains medical-debt information.
- **Related leverage:** The same chapter defines a large health-care facility to include licensed hospitals, hospital-operated or majority hospital-owned outpatient facilities, and licensed freestanding emergency departments; it bars interest and late fees and requires payment plans for qualifying balances. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 6, Chapter 25J, §§ 2502J, 2504J, and 2509J](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c025j/index.html).

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

- **Current charity-care rule:** Until the contingency stated in the 2025 session law is fulfilled, **16 Del. C. § 9311** requires persons subject to a Certificate of Public Review to perform and accept charity care to the extent required by the Board for people meeting Board criteria; DHSS enforces that requirement. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 16, § 9311](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title16/c093/).
- **Medical-debt notice and collection rules:** **6 Del. C. § 2503J** requires large health-care facilities to give uninsured patients written information about medical assistance at time of service and on each billing statement; for the listed emergency-department, admitted-hospital, and surgery patients, the notice must say they may qualify, explain how to apply, list local application-help organizations, and provide a facility contact. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 6, § 2503J](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c025j/index.html).
- **Collection leverage:** Under **6 Del. C. § 2505J**, covered creditors cannot use permissible extraordinary collection actions until 120 days after the first bill, must give at least 30 days' notice with financial-assistance information before such action, and must refund excess payments after financial assistance or charity care. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 6, § 2505J](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c025j/index.html).
- **Pending-application leverage:** The Code also shows an effective-upon-contingency version of **§ 2508J** that limits collection communications, litigation, and debt referral while the creditor knows a Title 16 financial-assistance determination or appeal is pending. Confirm the contingency and current implementation before citing that version. **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 6, § 2508J](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c025j/index.html).

## Hospital lien statute

- **Statute:** Delaware Hospital Liens, **25 Del. C. §§ 4301-4304**.
- **Source:** [Delaware Code, Title 25, Chapter 43](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title25/c043/index.html).
- **Scope:** A qualifying charitable hospital may hold a lien for reasonable hospital charges on the injured patient's claim, lawsuit, judgment, or settlement against a third party arising from the accident. It is not a general lien on the patient's home, wages, or ordinary bank account.
- **Notice:** To establish the lien, the hospital must file a written notice with the county prothonotary before payment to the injured person and send registered-mail copies to the injured person and known interested parties; a service affidavit is then filed. **25 Del. C. § 4302.**
- **Practical use:** This is most relevant to accident-related bills with a third-party recovery. Check both the hospital's charitable status and the statutory notice steps.

## Key Delaware-specific advantages

1. **Medical-debt reporting is prohibited.** Section 2507J bars both furnishing medical debt to a consumer reporting agency and a consumer reporting agency's inclusion of medical-debt information it knows or should know is present.
2. **The Medical Debt Protection Act restricts collection pressure.** Covered creditors cannot charge interest or late fees, must offer a payment plan for qualifying balances, and face a 120-day wait plus a 30-day warning before permissible extraordinary collection action.
3. **Delaware's balance-billing statutes supply a paper-trail lever.** For the state-regulated policies within § 3571S, an out-of-network provider's missing written disclosure or timely consent prevents balance billing, and a requested charge-range estimate is due within 3 business days.
4. **A hospital financial-assistance failure can be material to collection.** The Code's current and contingency provisions tie collection timing and notice to financial assistance; preserve every bill, notice, application, and decision.
5. **Hospital liens are limited to a third-party injury recovery.** The statutory lien is not a free-standing charge against ordinary assets, and it has filing and notice requirements.

## Verification gaps

Claims that could not be confirmed against an applicable primary source were left out rather than guessed at:

- **General hospital itemized-bill right:** The reviewed Delaware sources contained narrow itemization rules for treatment facilities and workers' compensation, but no verified general-hospital itemization-on-demand statute or response deadline. Confirm a hospital-specific rule before citing one.
- **Ground-ambulance protection:** The reviewed statute has a balance-billing carveout for specified volunteer or certified EMS providers. This pack does not claim a broader statewide ground-ambulance shield without a source that directly establishes one.
- **New Title 16 financial-assistance subchapter:** The official Code shows contingent versions of §§ 9311, 2505J, 2508J, and 2511J tied to 85 Del. Laws, c. 349. The enacted subchapter's operative date and implementing status must be confirmed before using its future standards in a letter or lawsuit.
