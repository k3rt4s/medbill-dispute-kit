# Vermont state pack

The state-law layer for Vermont medical-bill disputes. It identifies statutes and official complaint routes that may be useful for a Vermont patient; it does not determine coverage or replace individual legal advice.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify annually.

## Hospital itemization right

- **Statute:** [18 V.S.A. § 1852(a)(12)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/042/01852) gives a hospital patient the right to an itemized, detailed, understandable explanation of charges regardless of payment source, plus information about financial assistance and billing and collections practices.
- **Use in a letter:** Request that explanation and identify the account, service dates, and disputed line items. The statute does not state a response deadline. Its patient-rights notice must be distributed on admission and posted at nurse stations; noncompliance may support a complaint to the listed licensing agency or Board of Medical Practice under the same section.
- **Scope:** This is a hospital-patient right. This research did not verify that § 1852 applies to an independent professional bill that is not a hospital bill.

## Unfair claims settlement practices

- **Statute:** [8 V.S.A. § 4724(9)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/08/129/04724) treats specified conduct as an unfair claim-settlement practice when committed with a frequency indicating a business practice. Listed conduct includes misrepresenting coverage facts or policy provisions, failing to act reasonably promptly on claim communications, failing to investigate reasonably, and failing to attempt prompt, fair, equitable settlements when liability is reasonably clear.
- **Caveat:** The same statute says it does not create or imply a private cause of action, while preserving a cause that otherwise exists. Use it as the factual basis for a Vermont Department of Financial Regulation insurance complaint; do not present it as a standalone private damages statute.

## Bad-faith remedy, statute or common law

- **Common law:** In [*Bushey v. Allstate Insurance Co.*, 164 Vt. 399, 402–03 (1995)](https://law.justia.com/cases/vermont/supreme-court/1995/op95-069.html), the Vermont Supreme Court recognized a first-party bad-faith action by an insured. The insurer must have had no reasonable basis to deny policy benefits and must have known or recklessly disregarded that lack of a reasonable basis; a fairly debatable claim may be challenged while the insurer determines a reasonable basis.
- **Use in a letter:** Preserve the policy, EOBs, claim submissions, denial reasons, and communications. Describe the coverage issue and request a written explanation. This remedy concerns an insurer’s handling of an insured’s own claim; it does not by itself resolve a provider’s direct bill.

## State surprise-billing statute, if broader than the federal NSA

- **Verified Vermont-specific rule:** [33 V.S.A. § 6502](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/33/065/06502) prohibits a physician who agrees to treat a Medicare or General Assistance beneficiary from balance billing that beneficiary, subject to § 6503. It is limited by its terms and is not a generally applicable expansion of federal No Surprises Act protections.
- **Federal baseline:** CMS states that the No Surprises Act generally protects covered consumers from unexpected out-of-network bills for emergency services, certain nonemergency services at in-network facilities, and air ambulance services. [CMS medical-bill rights](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights)
- **Research result:** No Vermont statute broader than that federal baseline was verified for this pack. Verify plan type, facility status, and any asserted state protection before relying on this conclusion.

## Ground-ambulance protection

- CMS states that ground ambulance services generally are not covered by the federal No Surprises Act billing protections, unless state law supplies different rules. [CMS medical-bill rights](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights)
- No Vermont-specific ground-ambulance protection was verified in this research. Ask the carrier and ambulance provider for the coverage determination, network basis, and any available payment-assistance process before paying or disputing the charge.

## State insurance department contact

- **Agency:** Vermont Department of Financial Regulation, Insurance Division. The Division regulates insurance companies, agents, and adjusters and accepts consumer complaints involving major medical, dental, pharmacy benefits, and other coverage. [Insurance complaints](https://dfr.vermont.gov/consumers/file-complaint/insurance/insurance-complaints)
- **Complaint route:** File online or submit by mail or fax through the Division’s complaint process. For help, call **800-964-1784** or email [dfr.insuranceinfo@vermont.gov](mailto:dfr.insuranceinfo@vermont.gov). The published mailing address is Department of Financial Regulation, Insurance Consumer Services, 89 Main Street, Montpelier, VT 05620-3101. [Insurance complaints](https://dfr.vermont.gov/consumers/file-complaint/insurance/insurance-complaints)
- **Use:** Attach the bill, EOB, denial, policy language, itemization request, and a dated timeline. The Division notes that a provider filing for a particular patient needs the insured’s authorization before staff can contact the insurer.

## State attorney general consumer protection contact

- **Agency:** Vermont Attorney General’s Consumer Assistance Program (CAP). CAP provides consumer education, complaint mediation, and connections to resources. [CAP consumer help](https://ago.vermont.gov/cap)
- **Contact:** Call **800-649-2424** or email [ago.cap@vermont.gov](mailto:ago.cap@vermont.gov). Published address: Consumer Assistance Program, 109 State Street, Montpelier, VT 05609-1001. [CAP consumer help](https://ago.vermont.gov/cap)
- **Use:** CAP is a consumer complaint and mediation route for a provider, collector, or business dispute. It is not a substitute for a timely answer to a lawsuit or an insurance appeal.

## Small claims court, name, dollar limit, filing fee, attorney rules

- **Court:** File in the Civil Division of the Vermont Superior Court. The Judiciary describes small claims as a simple, informal procedure, and says filings may be made in the county where the plaintiff or defendant lives. [Vermont Judiciary small claims guide](https://www.vtcourts.gov/civil/suing-and-being-sued-small-claims)
- **Dollar limit:** The general small-claims limit is **$10,000**. A collection action for **medical debt** or consumer-credit debt cannot exceed **$5,000** in small claims. [12 V.S.A. § 5531](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/12/187/05531)
- **Filing fee:** As published by the Vermont Judiciary on 2026-09-07, the filing fee is **$65** for a claim of $1,000 or less and **$90** for a claim over $1,000. The Judiciary says a person who cannot afford the fee may request a waiver. [Vermont Judiciary small claims guide](https://www.vtcourts.gov/civil/suing-and-being-sued-small-claims)
- **Attorney rule:** A person, corporation, or other legal entity may be represented by an attorney but is not required to be. [12 V.S.A. § 5536](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/12/187/05536)

## Statute of limitations

- **Written and oral contract:** The kit’s Vermont cross-state row lists **six years** for both. [12 V.S.A. § 511](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/12/023/00511) supplies a six-year default for civil actions unless another provision applies.
- **Use:** Medical-billing claims can depend on the particular agreement and facts. Preserve the first bill, contract or admission materials, payment history, and communications; verify whether a more specific limitation or tolling rule applies before asserting a deadline.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

- **Statute:** [9 V.S.A. § 2466d](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/063/02466d) prohibits a credit reporting agency from reporting or maintaining in a consumer file information relating to medical debt. The statute defines medical debt to include debt from health care services and health care goods, with specified exclusions including veterinary services and certain general-purpose or secured credit.
- **Effective date and source-summary correction:** The current official page identifies § 2466d as added by **2025, No. 21, § 4**, effective July 1, 2025. This pack therefore uses § 2466d rather than the existing cross-state summary’s reference to “Act 76 (2023).”
- **Use:** If a consumer report contains covered medical-debt information, preserve the report and dispute it with the reporting agency using the report’s dispute process; the statute addresses reporting-agency treatment of the information, not the validity of the underlying bill.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

- **Policy and eligibility:** Each large health care facility must maintain a written financial-assistance policy. At a minimum it covers emergency and other medically necessary services and requires stated free-care, discount, and catastrophic-assistance levels based on household income and coverage status. [18 V.S.A. § 9482](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/221/09482)
- **Before seeking payment:** The facility must take specified steps before seeking payment, including determining available coverage, offering application information and help, and offering a financial-assistance application at no charge. For a qualifying patient, the facility or medical-debt collector must offer a payment plan whose monthly payment does not exceed five percent of gross monthly household income; it may not charge interest on debt owed by a qualifying patient. [18 V.S.A. § 9483](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/221/09483)
- **Notice:** Facilities must widely publicize the policy and application, including online and free paper copies. Billing statements must conspicuously state that income-based financial assistance is available, and collection attempts for debt from a large health care facility must include information about the relevant policy. [18 V.S.A. § 9484](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/221/09484)
- **Correction to existing summary:** The cross-state summary cites **18 V.S.A. § 9456** for FAP. Current § 9456 is a hospital-budget-review provision; the current FAP provisions are §§ 9482–9484. [18 V.S.A. § 9456](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/221/09456)

## Hospital lien statute

- **Statute:** [18 V.S.A. § 2251](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/051/02251) permits a Vermont hospital to file a lien on an accident victim’s damages recovery, subject to the statute’s conditions.
- **Patient-side limits:** A hospital has no lien if the patient has qualifying health insurance and provides proof no later than 90 days after discharge or death. A lien for deductible or coinsurance may be filed only at least 120 days after the hospital billed the health plan. A hospital recovering under the chapter bears a pro rata share of legal and administrative expenses, and the lien cannot exceed one-third of the patient’s net recovery.
- **Use:** If an accident settlement is involved, give the hospital proof of health insurance promptly and have counsel review any lien notice before settlement funds are distributed.

## Verification gaps

- No Vermont-specific ground-ambulance billing protection was verified. This pack relies only on CMS’s stated federal limitation and does not make an unverified claim that Vermont has no such rule.
- No generally applicable Vermont surprise-billing expansion beyond the federal No Surprises Act was verified. The pack includes the narrower Medicare/General Assistance physician rule in 33 V.S.A. § 6502 and leaves plan-specific analysis for local verification.
- The itemization statute expressly concerns hospital patients. Whether a particular bill is a hospital bill or an independent professional bill must be determined from the provider and billing entity.

## Key Vermont-specific advantages

- Hospital patients have a statutory right to an itemized, detailed, understandable explanation of charges and related financial-assistance and collections information under [18 V.S.A. § 1852](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/042/01852).
- Credit reporting agencies may not report or maintain covered medical-debt information under [9 V.S.A. § 2466d](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/09/063/02466d).
- Large health care facilities must offer a statutory financial-assistance framework, including free or discounted care criteria, a no-charge application, and, for qualifying patients, an income-capped payment plan without interest. [18 V.S.A. §§ 9482–9483](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/18/221)
- Hospital accident liens are limited when timely insurance proof is provided, and any lien is capped at one-third of the patient’s net recovery. [18 V.S.A. § 2251](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/051/02251)
