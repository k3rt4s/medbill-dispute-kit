All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify before use. A verification gap does not establish that the state has no applicable law.

## Hospital itemization right

A general patient-facing itemization deadline remains unverified. Do not fill [VERIFIED ITEMIZATION DEADLINE] from an estimate or transparency proposal. In the narrower hospital-lien context, the filed statement must include itemized charges under [N.D.C.C. § 35-18-03(7)](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t35c18.pdf), verified 2026-09-07. ## Unfair claims settlement practices

[N.D.C.C. § 26.1-04-03(9)](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t26-1c04.pdf), verified 2026-09-07, lists claims practices including misrepresentation, delayed acknowledgment and investigation failures, subject to its lack-of-just-cause and general-business-practice conditions. Cite the particular conduct in an insurance complaint. Whether this creates a standalone private claim was not resolved; do not represent it as settled.

Consumer Protection: (701) 328-3404, or 1-800-472-2600 within North Dakota. General inquiry email: <ndag@nd.gov>. ## Small claims court

Small Claims Court is a division of district court, with a $15,000 ceiling. The [court guide](https://www.ndcourts.gov/legal-self-help/small-claims), verified 2026-09-07, explains eligibility and warns that the defendant may remove the case to district court. [N.D.C.C. § 27-08.1-03](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t27c08-1.pdf), verified 2026-09-07, permits appearance without counsel; it does not impose an attorney ban. Section 27-08.1-04 provides fee-shifting to a prevailing plaintiff following the defendant's removal. The [court's June 23, 2025 fee notice](https://www.ndcourts.gov/news/north-dakota/legal-news/general-news/notice-of-fee-increases), checked 2026-09-07, states a $20 small-claims filing fee effective July 1, 2025. Confirm service costs separately.

## Statute of limitations

[N.D.C.C. § 28-01-16(1)](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t28c01.pdf), verified 2026-09-07, generally provides six years after accrual for express or implied contract obligations, subject to the stated exceptions. This matches the kit's ND written/oral 6/6 row. Classification, accrual, payment effects and exceptions require case-specific review; do not mechanically calculate a deadline from the table.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

A North Dakota medical-debt reporting prohibition remains unverified. Federal [15 U.S.C. § 1681i](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1681i), verified 2026-09-07, supplies a reinvestigation mechanism for disputed report information; it is not a blanket medical-debt reporting ban.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

Additional North Dakota hospital FAP requirements were not verified. Covered charitable hospitals have federal policy, charge and collection requirements under [26 U.S.C. § 501(r)(4)-(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/501), verified 2026-09-07. Request the actual hospital policy and eligibility criteria; do not invent an income threshold or assert there is no additional state rule.

## Hospital lien statute

[N.D.C.C. §§ 35-18-01 through 35-18-03](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t35c18.pdf), verified 2026-09-07, authorize liens for reasonable hospital services following accidental injury, attach them to specified claims and insurance proceeds, require notice and an itemized verified lien statement, and require filing no later than 30 days after services end. Section 35-18-08 separately addresses hospitalization insurance. Assess the actual lien and full chapter before alleging invalidity.

## Key North Dakota-specific advantages

1. A hospital-lien statement must expose itemized charges under § 35-18-03(7), a concrete document to inspect in a lien dispute.
2. Small-claims parties may appear without counsel under § 27-08.1-03, subject to the court's jurisdiction and procedures.
3. Section 27-08.1-04 provides attorney-fee protection to a prevailing plaintiff when a defendant removes the case to district court.

These are practical uses of the cited state procedures, not promises of recovery.
