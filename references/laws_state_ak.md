# Alaska state pack, research draft

The state-law layer for Alaska patients. This research draft is deliberately limited to authorities verified below; it does not convert a missing verification into an assertion that Alaska has no protection.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify annually. Unresolved Alaska-specific questions appear under "Verification gaps."

## Hospital itemization right

Alaska Stat. § 18.23.400(g)-(h) gives a prospective price-disclosure right, rather than a verified post-service itemized-bill deadline. For nonemergency services, a patient may request a good-faith estimate before service; the provider, facility, or insurer must supply it before service and no later than 10 business days after the request. A provider or facility estimate must include a plain-language description, procedure codes, facility fees, and identified or suspected additional billers. Section 18.23.400(k) authorizes the Department of Health to impose a civil penalty for noncompliance. [Official Alaska compilation](https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp?media=print&secEnd=18.30&secStart=18); [cross-checked current mirror](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-18/chapter-23/article-4/section-18-23-400/).

For an already-issued bill, request an itemized statement in writing, but do not state a state response deadline until it is independently verified. The statute does not make a provider or facility liable merely because an estimate differs from the later charge. [Alaska Stat. § 18.23.400(i)](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-18/chapter-23/article-4/section-18-23-400/).

## Unfair claims settlement practices

Alaska Stat. § 21.36.125(a) prohibits specified insurer claims practices, including misrepresenting facts or policy provisions, failing to acknowledge communications promptly, failing to investigate reasonably, and failing to seek prompt, fair, and equitable settlement where liability is reasonably clear. The statute expressly does not create or imply a private cause of action. Use it in a Division of Insurance complaint, not as a standalone damages claim. [Alaska Stat. § 21.36.125, official session-law text](https://www.akleg.gov/pdf/billfiles/SLAs/SLA%202000/CH%2097%20SLA%202000.pdf); [Alaska Supreme Court, *O.K. Lumber*](https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/1988/s-2182-1.html).

## Bad-faith remedy, statute or common law

Alaska recognizes an insured's claim for breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing against its insurer in tort as well as contract. The Alaska Supreme Court describes bad-faith claims as grounded in that implied covenant and distinguishes compensatory bad-faith relief from the higher showing required for punitive damages. [*State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Weiford*, 831 P.2d 1264, 1266 (Alaska 1992)](https://law.justia.com/cases/alaska/supreme-court/1992/s-4331-1.html).

This is an insurer-side remedy. It does not itself make a hospital or a debt collector liable for a disputed bill. Plan terms and ERISA may change the available remedy, so verify the plan type before using this theory.

## State surprise-billing statute, if broader than the federal NSA

The Alaska Division of Insurance's 2022 consumer material describes the federal No Surprises Act as the source of protection for covered emergency out-of-network care, including air ambulance, and covered nonemergency out-of-network services at an in-network facility. That dated material also discussed 3 AAC 26.110, but the official Alaska notice records removal of the 80th-percentile requirement effective January 1, 2024; that removed requirement is not a current rule to use in a letter. This research did not verify an Alaska patient-side surprise-billing statute broader than the federal Act. [Alaska Division of Insurance, *New Protections from Surprise Medical Bills* (2022 historical material)](https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/portals/11/pub/INS__NoSurprsiseActProtections_2022.pdf); [official repeal notice](https://aws.state.ak.us/OnlinePublicNotices/notices/View.aspx?id=211867).

Use the federal No Surprises Act for the federal categories. The scope of any current Alaska-specific patient balance-billing protection remains a verification gap.

## Ground-ambulance protection

The Alaska Division of Insurance states that the federal No Surprises Act generally does not apply to ground-ambulance services. This research did not verify a separate Alaska ground-ambulance balance-billing protection. Treat the Alaska-specific question as unresolved and verify locally before asserting a state-law protection. [Alaska Division of Insurance, *New Protections from Surprise Medical Bills*](https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/portals/11/pub/INS__NoSurprsiseActProtections_2022.pdf).

## State insurance department contact

**Alaska Division of Insurance, Consumer Services.** The Division says it investigates claim-handling delays, denials, and settlement issues within its regulatory authority. It lists Consumer Services at 550 W. 7th Ave., Suite 1560, Anchorage, AK 99501-3567; phone 907-269-7900 or 1-800-INSURAK in state outside Anchorage; and email consumerservices@alaska.gov. It cannot provide legal advice, negotiate settlements, or address matters it cannot legally enforce. [Division complaint page](https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/ins/Consumers/Complaints.aspx).

## State attorney general consumer protection contact

**Alaska Attorney General, Consumer Protection Unit.** The Unit accepts consumer complaints about unfair or deceptive practices, ordinarily attempts informal mediation, and uses complaints to identify patterns for possible enforcement. It does not act as a consumer's private attorney. The page lists 1031 West 4th Avenue, Suite 200, Anchorage, AK 99501-1994; phone 907-269-5200 or 1-888-576-2529 outside Anchorage; and email consumerprotection@alaska.gov. [Alaska Department of Law complaint page](https://law.alaska.gov/department/civil/consumer/cp_complaint.html).

## Small claims court

**Court:** Alaska District Court small claims procedure. **Limit:** $10,000, exclusive of costs, interest, and attorney fees, under Alaska Stat. § 22.15.040(a); a claimant with a larger claim may use small claims only by giving up the excess. **Filing fee:** $50 for a claim of $2,500 or less, $100 for a claim over $2,500, under Alaska Administrative Rule 9(c)(2), as published and read on 2026-09-07. **Attorney rule:** a lawyer is not required, but a party may have one; all parties must agree to use the simplified small-claims procedure. [Alaska Court System Small Claims Information Sheet, including § 22.15.040](https://public.courts.alaska.gov/web/forms/docs/sc-95.pdf); [Alaska Administrative Rule 9 fee schedule](https://www.courts.alaska.gov/rules/docs/adm.pdf).

## Statute of limitations

Alaska Stat. § 09.10.053 gives a **three-year** limitation period for an action upon an express or implied contract, subject to exceptions in § 09.10.040 or other law. This matches the existing kit row for AK, which lists three years for written contracts and open accounts. The existing row also marks partial payment and written acknowledgment as re-aging events; this draft did not independently verify those re-aging rules, so do not rely on them without further legal research. [Official Alaska Title 9 compilation](https://www.akleg.gov/statutesPDF/Title-9.pdf); [current statute mirror](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-9/chapter-10/).

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

The official Alaska Legislature page for HB 178, titled "Medical Debt: Information, Discrimination," displays the bill in House Finance with a status date of May 9, 2025. A pending bill is not an operative protection. This research does not identify a verified Alaska medical-debt credit-reporting statute; use the federal credit-reporting analysis only after checking the current law. ## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

This research did not verify an Alaska-specific financial-assistance-policy requirement. Tax-exempt hospital organizations remain subject to federal IRC § 501(r), including the written financial-assistance-policy requirement in § 501(r)(4). That federal obligation applies to tax-exempt hospital organizations, not automatically to every Alaska provider or hospital. [IRS, Financial assistance policies](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policies-faps).

Request the particular hospital's financial-assistance policy and application in writing. Do not represent a federal § 501(r) obligation as an Alaska state-law requirement.

## Hospital lien statute

Alaska Stat. § 34.35.450 gives a hospital, physician, or licensed special nurse furnishing services to a person with a traumatic injury a lien on the injured person's judgment, settlement, or compromise, to the extent of the reasonable value of the services. It is not a general lien on the patient's home, wages, or bank account. Section 34.35.460(a) sets filing and registered-mail notice requirements. Its alternative timing clauses require case-specific review; this pack does not resolve their application to a particular discharge or service date. [Official Alaska Title 34 compilation](https://www.akleg.gov/statutesPDF/Title-34.pdf); [current § 34.35.450 mirror](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-34/chapter-35/article-12/section-34-35-450/); [current § 34.35.460 mirror](https://law.justia.com/codes/alaska/title-34/chapter-35/article-12/section-34-35-460/).

## Key Alaska-specific advantages

1. **Prospective estimate with detail.** For scheduled nonemergency care, § 18.23.400 requires a requested estimate to identify procedure codes, facility fees, and known or suspected additional billers, creating a useful pre-service record.
2. **A regulator-enforced claims standard.** Section 21.36.125 supplies specific standards for a Division of Insurance complaint even though it is not a standalone private damages claim.
3. **A separate insurer bad-faith route.** An insured may have a common-law good-faith-and-fair-dealing claim against an insurer, subject to plan-specific limits.
4. **Low-cost small claims venue.** A disputed amount up to $10,000 can use the District Court's simplified procedure with the published $50 or $100 filing fee.
5. **Lien-perfection leverage after trauma.** A hospital lien reaches defined recovery proceeds, and § 34.35.460(a)'s text supplies concrete filing and notice language to compare with a lien demand.

## Verification gaps

The following could not be confirmed against a source this pack directly retrieved. Do not treat them as settled until confirmed independently:

- **Post-service itemized-bill right.** The verified Alaska authority establishes requested prospective estimates for nonemergency care. It did not establish a general post-service itemized-bill deadline or a private enforcement remedy.
- **Broader Alaska surprise-billing and ground-ambulance rules.** The Alaska Division of Insurance's 2022 source verifies federal Act material and is historical on the 80th-percentile requirement, which an official notice says was removed effective January 1, 2024. This research did not locate and directly verify a distinct current Alaska patient-side balance-billing statute.
- **Medical-debt credit reporting.** HB 178 was shown as pending on the official Legislature page. This research did not directly verify an enacted Alaska medical-debt credit-reporting provision.
- **State FAP requirement.** The federal § 501(r) obligation is verified for tax-exempt hospital organizations. This research did not directly verify a separate Alaska state FAP or charity-care mandate.
- **Re-aging.** The existing kit cross-state SOL row marks partial payment and written acknowledgment as re-aging events for Alaska, but this draft did not independently verify the controlling Alaska rule or its application to a medical-debt claim.
