# Maine state pack

The state-law layer for Maine patients. Before mailing a letter, verify each citation against the Maine Legislature's current text or with a local consumer-law attorney.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify annually.

## Hospital itemization right

- **Statute:** **22 M.R.S. § 1712**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 22 M.R.S. § 1712](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1712.html).
- **What it requires:** A licensed hospital must tell a patient or legal guardian in writing at discharge that an itemized bill is available on request. The request may be made at discharge or within **7 years** after discharge, and the hospital must provide the bill within **30 days** after the request. Hospital bills must itemize nursing-service costs.

Maine also requires a health care entity to give an uninsured or self-pay patient a requested good-faith estimate within the statutory time frames, with separately disclosed component prices and CPT codes. An insured patient may request a description of scheduled services and applicable codes. See [Maine Legislature, 22 M.R.S. § 1718-C](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1718-C.html).

## Unfair claims settlement practices

- **Statute:** **24-A M.R.S. § 2436-A(1)**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 24-A M.R.S. § 2436-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/24-A/title24-Asec2436-A.html).
- **Substance:** An injured person may bring a civil action against that person's own insurer for knowingly misrepresenting relevant facts or coverage provisions, failing to acknowledge and review a claim within a reasonable time after written notice, failing to affirm or deny coverage after its investigation, or, without just cause, failing to make a prompt, fair, and equitable settlement when liability is reasonably clear.

This is a private civil-action provision for conduct by the patient's own insurer, not a general claim against a provider or collector.

## Bad-faith remedy

- **Statute:** **24-A M.R.S. § 2436-A(1)-(3)**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 24-A M.R.S. § 2436-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/24-A/title24-Asec2436-A.html).
- **Remedy:** The same statute authorizes damages, costs and disbursements, reasonable attorney's fees, and interest on damages at **1.5% per month** for the enumerated conduct by the person's own insurer. It defines acting "without just cause" as refusing settlement without a reasonable basis to contest liability, damages, or injuries. The statute does not limit another cause of action.

Confirm plan type and any federal preemption issue with a qualified attorney before asserting a state-law insurer remedy.

## State surprise-billing statute

- **Statute:** **24-A M.R.S. § 4303-C**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 24-A M.R.S. § 4303-C](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/24-A/title24-Asec4303-C.html).
- **Substance:** Maine defines a surprise bill as covered out-of-network services at a network provider during a network-provider service or authorized procedure when the enrollee did not knowingly elect the out-of-network provider. For covered out-of-network emergency services, the enrollee's responsibility is limited to the in-network amount and the carrier must hold the enrollee harmless from additional amounts once that share is paid.

This pack does not represent Maine's statute as broader than the federal No Surprises Act in every scenario. Verify whether the state statute applies to the plan and service at issue before relying on it.

## Ground-ambulance protection

- **Statutes:** **24-A M.R.S. §§ 4303-C(2)(D-1), 4303-F**.
- **Sources:** [Maine Legislature, 24-A M.R.S. § 4303-C](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/24-A/title24-Asec4303-C.html); [Maine Legislature, 24-A M.R.S. § 4303-F](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/24-A/title24-Asec4303-F.html).
- **Verified state rule:** Maine specifies carrier reimbursement for covered ground-ambulance services, including an out-of-network reimbursement calculation, and bars prior authorization before listed ground-ambulance transports.

The federal No Surprises Act generally does not cover ground-ambulance billing protections. See [CMS, Know your Medical Bill of Rights](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights). The statutory text reviewed here does not resolve whether Maine's enrollee hold-harmless rule expressly bars a ground-ambulance provider from balance billing; see **Verification gaps**.

## Small claims court

- **Court name:** **Maine District Court, Small Claims session**.
- **Jurisdictional limit:** **$10,000**, exclusive of interest and costs, effective January 1, 2026. See [Maine Legislature, 14 M.R.S. § 7482](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec7482.html).
- **Court authority:** The District Court has jurisdiction, including limited monetary and equitable relief. See [Maine Legislature, 14 M.R.S. § 7481](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/14/title14sec7481.html).
- **Filing fee:** **$70**, as published in the court fee schedule effective March 9, 2026. Optional clerk-arranged service is **$15 per party**. See [Maine Judicial Branch, Court Fees Schedule](https://www.courts.maine.gov/adminorders/jb-05-26.pdf).
- **Attorney rules:** Attorneys are permitted, but not required. See [Maine Rules of Small Claims Procedure, Rule 16](https://www.courts.maine.gov/rules/text/mrscp%202024-11-01.pdf).

The Judicial Branch describes small claims as a District Court session and confirms that parties may represent themselves or hire an attorney. See [Maine Judicial Branch, Small Claims](https://www.courts.maine.gov/help/small-claims/index.html).

## Statute of limitations

- **Civil actions, including ordinary contract claims unless another statute supplies a different period:** **6 years after accrual**, **14 M.R.S. § 752**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 14 M.R.S. § 752](https://www.legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/14/title14sec752.html).

This matches the kit's existing Maine row: six years for both written and oral claims. A limitation period can depend on the particular claim and accrual facts; obtain local legal advice before allowing a deadline to expire.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

- **Statute:** **10 M.R.S. § 1310-H(4)(A)**.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 10 M.R.S. § 1310-H](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec1310-H.html).
- **Protection:** A consumer reporting agency may not report medical debt on a consumer report, and a medical creditor, debt collector, or debt buyer may not report a consumer's medical debt to a consumer reporting agency.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

- **Statute:** **22 M.R.S. § 1716-A**, effective July 1, 2026.
- **Source:** [Maine Legislature, 22 M.R.S. § 1716-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1716-A.html).
- **Protection:** Maine hospitals must provide free medically necessary services to eligible state residents with family income at or below **200% of the federal poverty level**, subject to department rules. The statute also requires written reasons for a full or partial charity-care denial and information about a fair-hearing request.
- **Payment plans:** For a patient with documented family income at or below **400% of the federal poverty level**, a hospital must offer a payment plan whose monthly out-of-pocket payment does not exceed **4%** of the patient's monthly family income that is not exempt from attachment or garnishment.

The statute also requires specified publication and application practices, including publicizing financial-assistance programs. Ask the hospital for its financial-assistance application and preserve the denial notice if one is issued.

## Hospital lien statute

- **Statutes:** **10 M.R.S. §§ 3411, 3412, 3412-A**.
- **Sources:** [Maine Legislature, 10 M.R.S. § 3411](https://www.legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec3411.html); [Maine Legislature, 10 M.R.S. § 3412](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec3412.html); [Maine Legislature, 10 M.R.S. § 3412-A](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/10/title10sec3412-A.html).
- **Substance:** A licensed hospital may have a lien for reasonable charges on an injured patient's related cause of action or recovery. The lien does not take precedence over the attorney's claim or contract for legal services, and does not apply to accident or health insurance-policy proceeds. To perfect the lien, the hospital must timely file and mail the statutory notice.
- **Patient-side limit:** The lien must be reduced by the patient's proportionate litigation or recovery costs, including reasonable attorney's fees, and must be satisfied on a just and equitable basis rather than as a priority lien.

This lien regime matters when the bill arises from an injury claim against a third party, rather than an ordinary medical-billing dispute.

## Key Maine-specific advantages

1. **A hospital itemization request stays available for seven years after discharge.** Section 1712 sets both the long request window and a 30-day response deadline.
2. **Maine's insurer claims-settlement statute carries a private civil remedy.** Section 2436-A authorizes damages, fees, costs, and 1.5% monthly interest for its enumerated conduct by the patient's own insurer.
3. **Maine expressly bars medical-debt reporting.** Section 1310-H(4)(A) restricts both consumer reporting agencies and medical creditors, collectors, and debt buyers.
4. **The 2026 hospital financial-assistance statute supplies concrete charity-care and payment-plan standards.** Section 1716-A ties free medically necessary care to 200% FPL and caps the described payment-plan amount for eligible patients.
5. **Hospital liens are not priority liens.** Section 3412-A requires a proportional reduction for recovery costs and a just-and-equitable resolution.

## Verification gaps

The following could not be confirmed to the standard required for a letter citation and are deliberately qualified rather than guessed:

- **Ground-ambulance balance-billing protection.** The current text of 24-A M.R.S. §§ 4303-C and 4303-F establishes carrier reimbursement rules for covered ground-ambulance services, but this research did not confirm that the enrollee hold-harmless language in § 4303-C expressly prohibits a ground-ambulance provider from balance billing. Do not represent a ground-ambulance balance-billing prohibition as verified from these sections without confirming the application to the particular plan and service.
- **A categorical conclusion that Maine's surprise-billing law is broader than the federal No Surprises Act.** Section 4303-C is a state surprise-bill protection, but this research did not compare every coverage, plan, and service category against federal law. Use the section's actual terms and verify plan applicability rather than asserting a universal broader-than-federal rule.
