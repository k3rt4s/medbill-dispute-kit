# Rhode Island state pack

The state-law layer for Rhode Island patients. Before mailing a letter, verify each citation against the Rhode Island General Laws' current text or with a local consumer-law attorney.

All citations verified against public sources as of 2026-09-07. Re-verify annually.

## Hospital itemization right

- **Statute:** **R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-17-19.1(11), (14)**.
- **Source:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 23-17-19.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-17/23-17-19.1.HTM).
- **What it requires:** A licensed healthcare facility must give a patient a summarized medical bill within **30 days of discharge**. On request, the patient must be furnished an **itemized copy** of the bill and may examine and receive an explanation of the bill. The summarized-bill and itemized-bill provision does not apply to residents of state-operated institutions and facilities.

## Unfair claims settlement practices

- **Statutes:** **R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 27-9.1-3, 27-9.1-4, 27-9.1-6**.
- **Sources:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 27-9.1-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE27/27-9.1/27-9.1-3.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 27-9.1-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE27/27-9.1/27-9.1-4_27-9.1-4.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 27-9.1-6](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE27/27-9.1/27-9.1-6.htm).
- **Substance:** The Act identifies claim-handling practices such as misrepresentation, unreasonable delay, inadequate investigation, failure to affirm or deny coverage after investigation, and not attempting a prompt, fair, and equitable settlement where liability is reasonably clear. Section 27-9.1-3 addresses conduct committed flagrantly and in conscious disregard of the chapter or frequently enough to show a general business practice.
- **Regulatory remedy:** After a hearing, the director may issue a cease-and-desist order and may impose the stated administrative penalties or suspend or revoke the insurer's license.

The provisions read establish regulatory remedies. This pack does not assert a private cause of action under the Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act; see **Verification gaps**.

## Bad-faith remedy

- **Statute:** **R.I. Gen. Laws § 9-1-33**.
- **Source:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 9-1-33](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE9/9-1/9-1-33.htm).
- **Remedy:** An insured may bring an action against the issuing insurer for a wrongful and bad-faith refusal to pay or settle a policy claim, or a wrongful and bad-faith refusal timely to perform contractual insurance obligations. The insured may seek compensatory damages, punitive damages, and reasonable attorney fees.

Confirm plan type and any federal preemption issue with a qualified attorney before asserting a state-law insurer remedy.

## Ground-ambulance protection

- **Statute:** **R.I. Gen. Laws § 27-41-73**.
- **Source:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 27-41-73](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE27/27-41/27-41-73.htm).
- **Verified protection:** The statute provides that an individual or group health-insurance contract, plan, or policy delivered, issued for delivery, or renewed in Rhode Island may not provide for a ground-ambulance copayment above **$50**. The provision defines covered ground-ambulance services and lists excepted coverages.

This is a copayment cap, not a verified general prohibition on ground-ambulance balance billing. The federal No Surprises Act generally does not cover ground-ambulance billing protections; see [CMS, Know your Medical Bill of Rights](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/your-patient-rights/medical-bill-rights/know-your-medical-bill-rights).

## Small claims court

- **Court name:** **Rhode Island District Court, Small Claims and Consumer Claims procedure**.
- **Jurisdictional limit:** **$5,000**, exclusive of interest and costs, for the listed contract, consumer-service, tax, and negotiable-instrument actions, at the plaintiff's election and waiver of appeal. See [Rhode Island General Laws, § 10-16-1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE10/10-16/10-16-1.htm).
- **Filing fee:** **$55** as published by the Rhode Island Judiciary. The court's current fee page also lists a one-time **$17.50** civil-case processing fee and a **$3.25** technology surcharge when applicable to an electronically filed or clerk-scanned civil case. See [Rhode Island Judiciary, District Court Civil Fees and Costs](https://www.courts.ri.gov/Courts/districtcourt/Pages/Civil-Fees-and-Costs.aspx).
- **Attorney rules:** An individual is not required to be represented by an attorney, but may engage one at the person's own expense. See [Rhode Island District Court Rules of Small Claims Procedure](https://www.courts.ri.gov/Courts/districtcourt/Documents/District_Rules_of_Small_Claims_Procedure.pdf).

## Statute of limitations

- **General civil actions, including ordinary contract claims unless another statute supplies a different period:** **10 years after accrual**, **R.I. Gen. Laws § 9-1-13(a)**.
- **Source:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 9-1-13](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE9/9-1/9-1-13.htm).

This matches the kit's existing Rhode Island row: ten years for both written and oral claims. A limitation period can depend on the particular claim and accrual facts; obtain local legal advice before allowing a deadline to expire.

## Medical-debt credit-reporting protections

- **Statutes:** **R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 6-60-2 through 6-60-5**, enacted in 2024 and effective January 1, 2025.
- **Sources:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 6-60-2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-60/6-60-2.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 6-60-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-60/6-60-3.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 6-60-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-60/6-60-4.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 6-60-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-60/6-60-5.htm).
- **Reporting ban:** A provider, licensed facility, or emergency medical transportation service may not furnish medical-debt information to a consumer reporting agency. A consumer reporting agency may not acquire, record, or report medical debt.
- **Collection protection:** A collector may not falsely represent that medical debt will appear in a consumer credit report or affect a credit score. During the stated pending insurance-review period, the statute also restricts reporting, collection communications, and litigation or arbitration.
- **Enforcement:** A noncompliant consumer reporting agency or debt collector that does not cure within 30 days after consumer notice is liable for **$10 per day** of noncompliance. After that period, the consumer may notify the Attorney General, who may seek an injunction and other relief.

## Charity care and state-level FAP requirements

- **Statute:** **R.I. Gen. Laws § 23-17.14-15**.
- **Source:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 23-17.14-15](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE23/23-17.14/23-17.14-15.htm).
- **State requirement:** As a condition of licensure, all licensed hospitals must meet statewide community needs for charitable care, meet standards for continuing uncompensated care and community benefits, and may not discourage people who cannot afford payment from seeking essential medical services or encourage them to seek essential services elsewhere. Hospitals must submit annual compliance reports, and the department may hold a hearing and impose the statute's referenced penalties.

The statute reviewed does not contain a patient-level **200% FPL** eligibility rule. Patients should request the hospital's financial-assistance policy and application, and nonprofit-hospital patients should also use the [federal IRC § 501(r) framework](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/501).

## Hospital lien statute

- **Statutes:** **R.I. Gen. Laws §§ 9-3-4, 9-3-5**.
- **Sources:** [Rhode Island General Laws, § 9-3-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE9/9-3/9-3-4.htm); [Rhode Island General Laws, § 9-3-5](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE9/9-3/9-3-5.htm).
- **Substance:** A hospital may have a lien for reasonable and necessary charges on the injured patient's recovery from a third party for an accident-related injury, subject to the statute's workers' compensation exclusion. The lien does not take precedence over an attorney lien.
- **Perfection:** The lien is ineffective unless the hospital files and mails the written statutory notice before payment of compensation to the patient or the patient's representative.

This lien regime matters when the bill arises from an injury claim against another person, rather than an ordinary medical-billing dispute.

## Key Rhode Island-specific advantages

1. **The itemization right is explicit.** Section 23-17-19.1 requires a summarized bill within 30 days of discharge and an itemized bill on request.
2. **An insured has an express bad-faith action against the issuing insurer.** Section 9-1-33 permits compensatory damages, punitive damages, and reasonable attorney fees for the stated conduct.
3. **The medical-debt reporting chapter has several patient-facing protections.** The 2024 law bars medical-debt reporting, limits specified collection conduct, and provides $10-per-day liability after a consumer notice and cure period.
4. **Covered-plan ground-ambulance copayments have a statutory ceiling.** Section 27-41-73 caps the copayment at $50 for the defined services, subject to the statute's coverage exceptions.
5. **The hospital lien is limited to an accident-related third-party recovery and sits behind an attorney lien.** A hospital must also satisfy the statutory notice steps before its lien becomes effective.

## Verification gaps

The following could not be confirmed to the standard required for a letter citation and are deliberately qualified rather than guessed:

- **A Rhode Island surprise-billing statute broader than the federal No Surprises Act.** Current official statutory research confirmed introduced historical bills concerning surprise out-of-network billing, but did not confirm an enacted current state provision that should be cited. This draft uses the federal rule and does not make an absence claim beyond that research result.
- **A private cause of action under the Unfair Claims Settlement Practices Act.** Sections 27-9.1-3, 27-9.1-4, and 27-9.1-6 establish prohibited practices and regulatory remedies, but this research did not confirm a private action under that chapter. Use the separately verified § 9-1-33 bad-faith action where its elements are met; do not plead the UCSPA as a private claim from this draft.
- **A patient-level income threshold in the Rhode Island charity-care statute.** The asserted cross-state 200% FPL threshold could not be verified in § 23-17.14-15, and § 23-17-12 concerns nursing-facility inspections. Do not cite either as a 200% FPL charity-care guarantee without a separately verified source.
